# 0008: Second-person checks on credit notes and transfers; who rejects after close

Date: 2026-10-08. Status: accepted.

## Context
Review of the cashier module found three gaps against FEATURES 5.11, 6.3 and 6.8:

1. A holder of `billing.approve_credit_note` could draft a credit note and approve it
   alone, so "with approval" (5.11) had no second person. With `open_refund=false` or
   `rebill`, one supervisor could reduce a receivable or reprice a frozen invoice.
2. A cashier supervisor who also works the till could confirm a bank transfer they took
   themselves, which defeats the bank check of 6.3.
3. Accountants hold `payments.reject_transfer`, but a transfer whose shift has closed is
   rejected into the rejecting user's own open shift (FLOW "الوردية المغلقة تُقفل",
   ARCHITECTURE 4.6, invariant 3), and accountants have no till. Every late rejection they
   tried failed with `SHIFT_NOT_OPEN`, and the message told them to open a shift they cannot
   open.

ADR 0009 already keeps refunds (`SELF_APPROVAL_NOT_ALLOWED`), shift reviews
(`SELF_REVIEW_NOT_ALLOWED`) and handover receipts (`HANDOVER_SELF_RECEIPT`) to two people.

## Decision
- **Credit notes.** `billing.approve_credit_note` refuses `CREDIT_NOTE_SELF_APPROVAL` (409)
  when the approver drafted the note. There is no break-glass exception: a center always has
  a second holder (supervisor, accountant, admin). The one-step cancellation of a billed line
  (`orders.cancel_line` → `billing.credit_service_line`, ARCHITECTURE 4.4 rule 5) keeps its
  single decision, because there the note is generated by the cancellation the approver
  decided; the money that leaves the drawer still needs a second person (the refund).
- **Transfers.** `payments.confirm_transfer` refuses `SELF_CONFIRMATION_NOT_ALLOWED` (409)
  to the user who took the payment and to the cashier of its shift. Rejection stays open to
  them: rejecting lowers collection and cannot hide missing money.
- **Late rejections.** The rule of FLOW and invariant 3 stays as written: a rejection after
  the shift closed is booked as a negative payment in the rejecting user's current open shift,
  and without one the service refuses `SHIFT_NOT_OPEN`. No shift-less reversal is introduced.
  Accountants keep `payments.reject_transfer` for transfers whose shift is still open (the
  effect then goes to that shift). The transfers queue says, per row, what stops the viewer
  (`self_recorded`, `reject_needs_open_shift`). The screen hides Confirm on the viewer's own
  transfers and disables Reject on a closed shift's transfer when the viewer has no open shift,
  with a hint: open your shift first (holders of `payments.open_shift`), or ask a cashier
  supervisor with an open shift (everyone else).

## Consequences
- An accountant who finds a bounced transfer from a closed shift hands it to a supervisor
  with a till. This is the cost of keeping every drawer effect in an open shift.
- A supervisor working the desk asks another checker to confirm transfers they took.
- Tests: `apps/billing/tests/test_review_fixes.py` and `apps/payments/tests/test_api.py`
  cover each refusal through the API for the supervisor and the accountant.
